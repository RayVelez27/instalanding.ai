03 — Principles

What we hold to

Six commitments that decide how we underwrite, govern and exit, written down so founders can hold us to them.

  1. IRetention is the thesis

    Net revenue retention tells the truth about a product. We underwrite it before we underwrite growth.

  2. IIFounders keep the pen

    We back the people who built the company and add operators around them, not over them.

  3. IIILeverage is a tool, not a plan

    Our capital structures are built to survive a bad year, not to flatter a good one.

  4. IVOperators in the room

    Every platform has a former CEO, CTO or CFO from our bench on its board from the first day.

  5. VTransparency both ways

    Founders see our model. Limited partners see our marks. Nobody is surprised in a board meeting.

  6. VIBuilt to be bought twice

    We exit businesses the next owner can keep growing, which is why our buyers keep calling back.

11 — For founders

Questions founders ask first

Something else on your mind? Ask a partner directly

Do we have to sell a majority stake?

No. Around a third of our platforms began as significant minority investments, with board seats and protective rights rather than control.

How long do you usually hold a company?

Four to seven years on average. We size each fund so we are never forced to sell a business that is still compounding.

What happens to the leadership team?

In most deals the founder stays as CEO and rolls meaningful equity. We add operators where the team asks for them, and we say so before signing.

How much leverage do you use?

Less than the market average. We stress-test every structure against a year of zero growth before we commit to it.

How quickly can you move?

A term sheet within three weeks of a first meeting is typical, and closing within sixty days of signing it.
