Header & menu

A 64px bar (72px from 768px): the square-and-arch mark with the wordmark in Instrument Sans 500 over a mono kicker, pill-shaped text links (the current one on a 5% ink ground, hover turns blue) and a compact ink button. 03 · type

Type, eyebrows & labels

Instrument Sans at 400 sets every heading, light and tight (-.035em on the display, -.03em on h2s), with the first word of the display in --lw-muted. Urbanist carries copy: --lw-lead (17px/1.65) at 70% ink. IBM Plex Mono does every small label: eyebrows in parentheses, tracked .14em in capitals and muted (a <b> inside turns blue), counters, dt labels, hints and the labels drawn into the SVG (.lw-lbl), which read like a drawing's annotations: scales, levels, dimensions.

04 · lines

Line weights & dimensions

The drawing language as a kit. Every stroke is a <path class="lw-st lw-s?" pathLength="1"> with a weight class: outline (.lw-so, 1.5px), line, entourage (trees and people), window, detail, guide (blue), surface, hatch (.lw-sh, .5px), plus dashed blue axes (.lw-sx); each weight is a token, and --lw-sw scales a whole drawing. Section fills are a 45° blue hatch pattern. Annotations are mono .lw-lbl text: dimension lines with ticks and rotated values, level marks (±0.00, +27.00), a north point, a scale bar and a DETAIL callout in the hero lens's corner marks.

05 · hero

Drawing hero & blueprint lens

Dot-grid paper, a light Instrument Sans display (--lw-display, up to --lw-display-xl from 1280px, where the copy moves over the drawing's right half), a lead, the ink button and a link, and a mono hint. The drawing is an amphitheatre in the page's own ink line set, wider than the screen, that draws itself stroke by stroke over three seconds: ground, outline, bays, then trees and people, with blue perspective guides and a dimension. A blue scan line sweeps it once, then a 300×190 blueprint lens (blue ground, white grid, the same drawing in white) roams on its own and follows the pointer, with DETAIL A, 1:200 above it and live X/H coordinates in metres below.

( What it does )

An assistant that knows the ground

From a bathing house for winter swimmers to a library for forty thousand neighbours, Linework starts every project with the site, the light and the people who will use it, then works beside you from the first sketch to the final handover.

01 / Site and concept

Upload a plot and get sun paths, wind, setbacks and neighbour heights mapped, then compare massing options against the brief.

02 / Drawings and detail

Plans, sections and elevations drafted from your sketches and kept in step with every change, down to joints at 1:5.

03 / Masterplanning

Test streets, courtyards and whole neighbourhoods around the walk between them, with daylight and density checked as you draw.

Cultural

Arkaden Sculpture Gallery

A long vaulted arcade for sculpture, where the sun moves across the floor like the hand of a clock.

Arkaden is a single room, 46 metres long, built for a private collection of twentieth century sculpture. Seven barrel vaults rest on a rhythm of lime plastered arches, and every bay faces south through a deep reveal.

Linework simulated the sun path for a full year before the architects fixed the depth of the windows. In the morning the light lands on the floor, at midday it climbs the walls, and in the late afternoon it reaches the sculptures at the far end of the gallery.

The building is made from a short list of materials: hand pressed brick, lime plaster and polished limestone, chosen to age slowly and to ask for almost nothing in return.

10 · process

Stage drawing & stepper

One drawing built in five stages: site and brief, massing, structure, facade and detail, then trees and people. Each stage's strokes draw in after the last, the current stage's lines turn blue and settle back to pencil grey, and labels (N, SITE 1:200, levels, spans) fade in. A frosted bar gives Stage 0n / 05 and the stage name; a strip of five 3px bars (blue for the current, pale blue for done) steps through it. Beside it, the five stages as mono numbers, h3s and leads with a status pill, the current at full strength. .lw-compact (as here) drives it from the steps or a click on a stage and folds the other stages to their titles; without it, a sticky drawing follows the full list as you scroll.

From the first walk to the last brick

Site and brief

  1. 01

    Site and brief

    Drop in a plot, a few photos and your notes. Linework maps the sun, the wind, the setbacks and the neighbours, and drafts a brief you refine with your client.

    Minutes, not weeks

  2. 02

    Concept and massing

    Ask for options and it sketches simple volumes, testing each against the plot, the light and the street. Keep the ones you like and send the rest back.

    Dozens of options an hour

  3. 03

    Grids, spans and floor depths are proposed early and checked with your engineers, so the structure becomes part of the architecture instead of something hidden behind it.

    Shared with your engineers

  4. 04

    Every sheet stays in sync

( Why Linework )

Made by architects, for the long table covered in drawings.

Linework was started in Copenhagen by a small team of architects and engineers who were tired of redrawing the same detail for the fifth time. It works the way a good studio does: site first, light first, and drawings that stay true to what gets built.

It never replaces the architect. You make every decision and sign every sheet. Linework does the measuring, the redrawing and the checking, so you have time to draw a hundred versions before you draw it once.

  1. 01

    Light first

    Every option is tested against the sun at every hour and in every season before you see it.

  2. 02

    Materials that age well

    Brick, timber, lime and stone come with real ageing and carbon data, not just a texture.

  3. 03

    You stay the architect

    Every suggestion is shown, explained and reversible. Nothing reaches a drawing without your say.
