02 · type

Cut letters & ransom titles

Titles are cut out, never set. A cut letter is <span class="pu-cut pu-e0..e3"><i>: the span carries the size, tilt (rotate) and offset (translate) inline and casts --pu-drop-scrap; the <i> carries the paper (paper, yellow, pink, red or ink), the face, and a clip-path edge (--pu-cut-0..3) over the paper fibre (tex-paper.png). Rotate the edges and faces so no two neighbours match, and keep tilts within ±5deg. Inside .pu-hl the letters land one by one (--pu-i × --pu-land-stagger). Section titles cut whole words instead, padded scraps at --pu-h2-scrap. The italic Playfair line with its drawn yellow underline (.pu-uline) and the Courier eyebrow, lead and facts carry the rest.

03 · buttons

Tilted buttons & links

A button is a cut scrap in a link: <a class="pu-btn"> tilted -2deg to -3deg inline, holding a .pu-cut.pu-e0 whose <i> is the yellow paper with Archivo Black capitals at .05em and a + or an arrow. Ink paper is the second button. Hover lifts it by --pu-hover-lift over --pu-dur-hover. Text links are Courier Prime: .pu-ghostlink sits on a 2px dashed white underline that turns yellow, .pu-navlink is plain. A spent control drops to --pu-spent paper with --pu-spent-ink; a destructive one is a round red scrap.

04 · labels

Time chips & torn labels

Every photo is named, never captioned: a black Anton time chip (.pu-chip, tilted -5deg on its own clip) tucked over a torn-paper strip (.pu-s1, the torn strip tex-strip-1.png stretched to fit) with the name in Courier Prime 700, wrapped in .pu-nsh, which casts --pu-drop-label and carries will-change: filter. Keep that: without its own layer Chrome sometimes repaints the strip but not the words on it. A cut number tucked under an ink tab counts photos; the chip alone marks a volume.

05 · scraps

Paper scraps, tape & torn edges

Surfaces are paper, not cards: a .pu-ppr scrap (paper, yellow or pink, the fibre multiplied over it) with one of three torn outlines (.pu-rag, .pu-rag2, .pu-rag3: --pu-rag-1..3), inside a .pu-prt that casts --pu-drop-print by that outline. Tilt each by ±1.5deg. Tape is three strips (.pu-tape-1..3, 96×23 by default, warmed by --pu-tape-filter) placed absolutely across an edge at a few degrees; scale them up to pin a whole sheet. Photos are .pu-ph tiles cut by the same rag edges, on --pu-photo-blank until the picture loads.

06 · prints

Taped photo prints

A photo print (.pu-print.pu-ppr): a --pu-print border 11px wide around a .pu-ph, the fibre multiplied over it, cast by --pu-shadow-print, held by one strip of tape (.pu-tape.pu-stick) across its top or a corner. Prints fly in from off the page: give each --pu-dx, --pu-dy and --pu-spin (where it comes from and how it turns) and an animation-delay, and it lands over --pu-dur-print on --pu-ease-print; its tape sticks 0.62s after its delay. Tilt prints -6 to 5deg.

07 · stamp

Stamp & postmark

A perforated stamp (.pu-stamp, 126px: a radial-gradient mask punches the edge every 11px) holding a photo tile, with a rubber postmark (two rings and four wavy cancel lines, an inline SVG in ink at 50%) struck across its left edge. It drops in on --pu-ease-stick at 2.15s, the last thing onto the hero: set animation-delay to place it elsewhere.

08 · marquee

Tape marquee

A black tape strip across the bottom of the hero: --pu-ink over the paper fibre, tilted --pu-marquee-tilt with slightly cut ends, Archivo Black capitals at .14em scrolling left over --pu-dur-marquee, each phrase followed by yellow scissors. The track repeats its phrases four times and moves by a quarter, so it loops without a seam. Place it absolutely at the foot of a section that hides overflow.

10 · collage

Paste-up collage

The hero's picture: a collage on six parallax layers (data-pu-depth 6 to 36 px of travel). Two loose sheets, white and pink (.pu-paper.pu-ppr, cast by --pu-shadow-paper), settle in tilted -6deg and 5.5deg; three prints fly in from off the page one after another, each held by a strip of tape that sticks a beat later; the stamp drops in last. Fixed at 740×690 and zoomed down at each breakpoint.

11 · hero

Paste-up hero

The opening: the header, then copy on the left (an eyebrow with an ink chip, a cut-letter title that lands letter by letter over an italic Playfair line with a drawn yellow underline, the lead, the yellow button beside a dashed link, and three facts with yellow icons) and the paste-up collage on the right, over a soft light, with the tape marquee across the foot. A fine pointer drifts the stage's layers. One column under 1020px.
